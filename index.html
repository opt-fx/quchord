Routing

Send QuChord's output to your synth or sequencer, and switch off any direct route from the keyboard to the same place, or you'll hear the single note as well as the chord. If your keyboard has an MPE mode, turn it off: QuChord sends each chord on one channel. Voice limit trims big chords to fit your synth's polyphony, dropping the least important notes first (doubled notes, then the 5th, then extensions) and keeping the root, 3rd and 7th.

Switch Strum on to spread the chord's notes out in time like a strummed guitar: 15–40 ms is subtle, 80 ms+ is a harp-like roll. Tempo sync sets the gap as a note length instead, so a 1/16 strum on a 4-note chord rolls out over one beat in time with the track. Set Clock in (Routing) to your sequencer's port to follow its tempo, or type a tempo or tap it in. Latch keeps the chord sounding after you let go, until you play the next one. It's handy for holding a pad while you tweak the synth. Velocity: if your keys or pads need hitting hard, use As played with the Soft or Softer curve and raise Minimum. The readout under the chord name shows what comes in and what goes out. Pass CC / bend forwards the keyboard's pressure, aftertouch and pitch bend messages so modulation still works.

Chord source

Number keys 1–8 select a slot.

Chord memory stores a chord's shape (the gaps between its notes), not its actual pitches. Learn C–E–G and every key plays a major triad built on itself. The small numbers under each slot are those gaps in semitones: 0·4·7 is a major triad, and 0·3·7 is minor.

Every key plays the chosen chord, with the key you press as its root (the note the chord is named after). Hover a chord for a description. Moving a single chord shape around like this is the classic house, techno and dub technique, and it sounds great on a Prophet.

No chords: each key plays just its own note, with the Octave setting from Voicing applied. Use it with the Arpeggiator for basslines and sequences. Unlike Bypass, the arpeggiator, octave shift and velocity settings still work.

Chord size
Off-scale keys

Every key plays a chord built only from notes in the chosen key, so everything you play fits together. The roman numerals give each chord's position in the key: I is built on the 1st note of the scale, V on the 5th, and so on. Uppercase means major, lowercase means minor, ° means diminished and ø means half-diminished. Because the numbers stay the same in any key, songs get described this way. Try I–V–vi–IV (countless pop songs), ii–V–I (jazz), or i–VI–III–VII in a minor key. Tap a chord above to hear it.

Inversion changes which chord note is at the bottom: same chord, different colour. Open spacing spreads the notes over a wider range for a bigger, lusher sound, and Drop 2 is a classic jazz piano voicing. Smooth picks whichever inversion is closest to the previous chord, so changes glide instead of jumping around. Melody puts the key you press on top and builds the chord underneath, so you can play a tune with one finger and hear it harmonised. Choose whether your note is the chord's root, 3rd or 5th (in Scale chords it uses the chord from the key). Bass: Pedal holds one low note while the chords change above it, either while you play or as a constant drone. Range folds every chord into a window, so chords stay in the same register wherever you play on the keyboard. Melody overrides Inversion and Range.

Arp and Euclid both run from the notes QuChord would play, so they follow the chord you hold. The arpeggiator plays whatever QuChord would play (the chord's notes, or the keys you hold in Single notes mode) one at a time, in time with the tempo. For a bassline: choose Single notes, hold one key, set Octaves to 2 and try Up at 1/16. Hold two or three keys to make a pattern from them, and turn on Latch to keep it running hands-free. With a port chosen in Clock in (Routing), it locks to that MIDI clock, starts new notes on the next step and restarts its pattern when the sequencer starts. Without one, it uses the manual tempo in Performance. Gate sets note length, and Swing delays every second step for shuffle. Euclid shows four lanes in place of the keyboard, with a ring for each lane here. Each lane spreads its Hits as evenly as possible over its Steps (3 hits in 8 steps is the classic tresillo), Rotate shifts where the pattern starts, and Note picks which chord note it plays: Low, 2nd, 3rd, Top, Cycle (each hit moves up the chord) or Random. Give lanes different lengths and they drift against each other, so the pattern keeps evolving. Chance lets hits drop out at random. Rate and Swing apply to all lanes. The dice randomise a lane, or every lane that's on (or the arpeggiator in Arp mode); press R to roll. Lane 1 stays a sparse bass lane, so a roll keeps a groove underneath.
